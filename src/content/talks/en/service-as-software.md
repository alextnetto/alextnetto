---
title: "Explaining Service-as-Software: why Silicon Valley is now paying attention to this thesis"
order: 1
---

For a long time, software was sold as a tool: the company pays for the license and the team does the work. With AI agents, a new generation of software delivers the finished work and charges for the outcome. Not the reconciliation system, but the reconciliation itself.

The math that caught investors' attention comes from Alex Rampell, general partner at a16z, in October 2025: the US labor market moves roughly US\$13 trillion a year, against roughly US\$300 billion for all SaaS worldwide. Y Combinator added AI-native service companies to its 2026 Requests for Startups. In this talk I explain the thesis, separate where it already works from where it is still a bet, and translate what changes for companies that buy and sell services.

### Who it is for

- Business owners and executives who outsource services or sell services by the hour.
- Founders and investors assessing where AI changes the economics of an industry.
- Technology and innovation leaders who need to bring the thesis to their board.

### What the audience takes away

- A simple way to tell software that helps the team from software that delivers the work.
- Criteria to identify which processes in the operation can already be bought by outcome.
- Questions to ask vendors at the next contract renewal.

### Outline

1. From tool to delivered work: what changes when software charges per outcome instead of per seat.
2. Why now: what AI agents have become able to do.
3. The math that drew the funds in: labor versus software, and who is betting on it.
4. Where it already works and where it is still a promise.
5. The effect on the market: what happens to those who sell hours and those who buy services.
