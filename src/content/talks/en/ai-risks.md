In May 2023, the CEOs of OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Anthropic signed, along with hundreds of researchers, a one-sentence statement: mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside pandemics and nuclear war. At the same time, many people treat the subject as science fiction or a marketing ploy.

This talk organizes the debate: what the risks are, who takes each one seriously and why, what already has evidence and what is still speculation. I come from cybersecurity, a field where thinking about the worst case is part of the job. That is the lens I bring to the topic: no panic, no dismissal.

### Who it is for

- Leaders, boards and risk managers who want a serious view of the subject.
- Universities, schools and technology events.
- Any audience that wants to understand the debate without hype or denial.

### What the audience takes away

- A map of the risks, from the most concrete to the most speculative, with the strongest sources for each.
- Criteria to separate well-founded warnings from alarmism.
- The same security questions at company scale: what to check before giving an AI agent autonomy.

### Outline

1. Why the subject left the labs and reached governments.
2. Risks in layers: malicious use (cyberattacks, fraud, biological weapons), failures and accidents, concentration of power, impact on work, and loss of control.
3. What already has evidence: deceptive and self-preserving behavior documented by the labs themselves in test environments.
4. Where serious experts disagree, and why.
5. What is being done: alignment and interpretability research, lab safety policies, and regulation.
6. From humanity to your company: agents with too many permissions, data leaks and fragile vendors.
